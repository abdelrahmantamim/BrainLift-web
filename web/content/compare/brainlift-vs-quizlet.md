Quizlet is a long-running flashcard platform built around study sets, many of them made and shared by other students. BrainLift is a newer iOS and web app that turns your own notes, photos, PDFs, or a topic into quizzes and flashcard decks, then builds review decks from the questions you miss. Both help you practice recall, but they start from different places.

## The short version

If you already have the material, such as lecture notes, a textbook chapter, or a stack of handwritten pages, BrainLift is built to turn that into practice quickly. You snap a photo or upload a file, pick a difficulty and how many questions you want, and you get a quiz and a deck. Wrong answers can become a flashcard deck with explanations, so your next session targets what you actually got wrong.

Quizlet clearly wins on reach. Its library of shared sets means you can often find a deck for a common course without making anything yourself. It runs on Android as well as iPhone and the web, many of your classmates probably already use it, and teachers can run classroom games with Quizlet Live. If those things matter more than generating questions from your own files, Quizlet is the safer choice.

## Where Quizlet is the better choice

If your course is popular, someone may already have made the set you need. Searching Quizlet for a textbook chapter or a common vocabulary list can save you the work of building cards at all. BrainLift has no public library, so every deck starts with your own material or a topic you type in.

Quizlet is also the practical pick if you use an Android phone or study in a group where everyone shares sets. Familiarity matters: when a teacher posts a Quizlet link or a study group already has a shared folder, joining in is easier than moving everyone to a new app. Teachers who want live classroom games will find Quizlet Live built for that.

Finally, Quizlet has been around for many years, and BrainLift launched in March 2026. If you want a tool with a long track record and a large community, that history counts for something.

## Where BrainLift is the better choice

BrainLift is strongest when the content only exists in your notes. A photo of a whiteboard, a page of handwriting, or a PDF of lecture slides can become a multiple-choice quiz and a flashcard deck in one step. If you have been meaning to make cards from your camera roll, our guide to [studying from photos of your notes](/blog/study-from-photos-of-notes/) walks through the process.

The mistakes loop is the other big difference. After a quiz, "practice my mistakes" collects the questions you missed into a new deck, each with an explanation, and those cards go into spaced review. You can also open the AI tutor chat to ask follow-up questions about a topic, and it can draw charts and diagrams when a picture helps.

If motivation is your weak spot, BrainLift adds XP, levels, daily quests, streaks, and achievements on top of review. Those features do not make the studying itself different, but they can make it easier to show up every day. As with any AI tool, check generated questions against your source, since AI-generated content can contain mistakes.

## Pricing

BrainLift is free to start with usage limits. Premium costs $6.99 per month, $49.99 per year, or $2.99 per week, as listed on the App Store.

Quizlet offers a free plan and paid plans, including Quizlet Plus. We could not confirm current prices on Quizlet's own site, so check the [official Quizlet upgrade page](https://quizlet.com/upgrade) for today's pricing.

## Switching or using both

You do not have to pick one. A common setup is to keep using Quizlet for shared class sets and vocabulary lists, then use BrainLift for the material that only exists in your notes or slides. That way you get the community library and the generated practice from your own files.

If you want to move your own Quizlet cards into BrainLift, the simplest route is to copy the terms into a typed note and generate a quiz or deck from it. ## FAQ

### Can I find pre-made study sets in BrainLift like on Quizlet?

No. BrainLift does not have a public library of pre-made sets. You create quizzes and decks from your own notes, files, or a topic, and you can share them with friends by link or QR code.

### Is BrainLift available on Android?

Not at this time. BrainLift runs on iPhone, iPad, and the web, so Android users can use the web app in a browser. Quizlet has a native Android app.

### Are AI-generated questions always correct?

No. AI-generated content can contain mistakes in either app. Compare questions against your notes or textbook, especially before an exam, and edit or skip anything that looks wrong.
