The MCAT gives you a passage you have never seen and expects you to bring biochemistry, physics, and psychology to it in seconds. Content review only pays off if the facts come back quickly under time pressure. That takes months of retrieval, not one pass through a review book.

## Where MCAT prep breaks down

Plenty of pre-meds download a flashcard program, spend a weekend on add-ons and settings, and stall. You need cards on day one, not a configuration project. BrainLift turns your notes into a deck straight away, with self-rated spaced review already built in.

### The same mistakes show up on every full-length

Missing enzyme kinetics on one practice test is normal. Missing it on three means the gap never got fixed. Generate a quiz on the topics that cost you points, and whatever you miss again goes through "practice my mistakes" into a deck with explanations you review until it stops being wrong.

## What a generated quiz looks like for the MCAT

**1. A competitive inhibitor affects Michaelis-Menten kinetics how?**
A) Km increases, Vmax unchanged (correct)
B) Km unchanged, Vmax decreases
C) Both decrease
D) Km decreases, Vmax increases
*Why:* The inhibitor competes for the active site, so more substrate is needed to reach half of Vmax, but enough substrate still reaches the same maximum.

**2. Where does oxidative phosphorylation take place in eukaryotic cells?**
A) Cytosol
B) Mitochondrial matrix
C) Outer mitochondrial membrane
D) Inner mitochondrial membrane (correct)
*Why:* The electron transport chain and ATP synthase are embedded in the inner membrane.

**3. In Pavlov's experiment, after conditioning, the bell is the:**
A) Unconditioned stimulus
B) Conditioned stimulus (correct)
C) Unconditioned response
D) Conditioned response
*Why:* The bell was neutral at first and became a conditioned stimulus once it was paired with food.

| Front | Back |
|---|---|
| Noncompetitive inhibitor: effect on Km and Vmax | Km unchanged, Vmax decreases |
| Henderson-Hasselbalch equation | pH = pKa + log([A-]/[HA]) |
| Net ATP from glycolysis per glucose | 2 ATP (plus 2 NADH) |
| Ohm's law | V = IR |
