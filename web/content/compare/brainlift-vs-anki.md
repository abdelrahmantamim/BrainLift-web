Anki is a free, open-source flashcard program that schedules reviews with spaced repetition and gives you deep control over cards, decks, and settings. BrainLift is an iOS and web app that generates quizzes and flashcard decks from your notes, photos, PDFs, or a topic, then turns your wrong answers into review cards. Anki is a toolkit you shape yourself, and BrainLift is a ready-made study flow.

## The short version

Anki clearly wins for power users. The desktop app and AnkiDroid are free, it is open source, it supports add-ons, and it offers the FSRS scheduler as an option alongside its older SM-2 based algorithm. Large community decks exist for subjects like medicine, so many students start with a shared deck instead of writing cards. If you are willing to learn its settings, Anki can do almost anything a flashcard system can do.

BrainLift is for the student who would rather spend that time studying. You do not write cards one by one. You upload a PDF or snap a photo, pick difficulty and question count, and get a multiple-choice quiz plus a flashcard deck. Wrong answers flow into a "practice my mistakes" deck with explanations, and an AI tutor chat is there when a card is not enough.

## Where Anki is the better choice

If you plan to memorize a large body of facts over months or years, Anki's control is a real advantage. You can tune scheduling, design card templates, use cloze deletions, and extend the app with add-ons. Switching on FSRS lets the scheduler adapt more closely to your own review history.

Cost is another clear win. Anki on desktop and AnkiDroid are free, and AnkiMobile is a one-time $24.99 purchase rather than a subscription. You can study on your own device and sync through the free AnkiWeb service. For medical students in particular, well-known community decks mean you can start with a complete deck on day one.

Anki is also open source and has been maintained for many years, which matters if you want a tool you can count on for a long program. If that sounds like you, Anki is a strong pick, and we would recommend it.

## Where BrainLift is the better choice

Anki asks you to build or find your cards. BrainLift builds them from what you already have. A PDF of slides, a photo of handwritten notes, or a typed outline becomes a quiz and a deck in one step, with the difficulty and number of questions you choose. If you have felt stuck in Anki setup instead of studying, our look at [MCAT Anki alternatives](/blog/mcat-anki-alternatives/) covers when a guided app makes more sense.

BrainLift also tests you in more than one format. Multiple-choice quizzes check whether you can pick the right answer among close options, and flashcards check recall. When you miss a quiz question, "practice my mistakes" turns it into a card with an explanation, so the fix is already written. For background on combining these methods, read [active recall vs spaced repetition](/blog/active-recall-vs-spaced-repetition/).

The extras are aimed at consistency: XP, levels, daily quests, streaks, and achievements, plus an AI tutor chat that can explain with charts and diagrams. AI-generated content can contain mistakes, so check new cards against your source before you rely on them.

## Pricing

BrainLift is free to start with usage limits. Premium is $6.99 per month, $49.99 per year, or $2.99 per week, as listed on the App Store.

Anki's desktop app and AnkiDroid are free. AnkiMobile for iPhone and iPad is a one-time purchase of $24.99 on the App Store. See [the official Anki site](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) for downloads.

## Switching or using both

Using both is common. Some students keep their long-term Anki collection and use BrainLift to quickly generate quizzes from a new lecture or a chapter they have not carded yet. Others use BrainLift's mistake decks to find weak areas, then add the most important facts to Anki for long-term review.

Anki can import plain text files with fields separated by tabs, commas, or semicolons, so a list of questions and answers you write out can go into Anki that way. Medical students can also see our page for [med students](/for/med-students/).

If you want to see how much setup time you can skip, [create a free BrainLift account](/auth/register) and generate a quiz from one lecture.

## FAQ

### Does BrainLift use FSRS like Anki?

No. BrainLift uses SM-2 style scheduling where you rate how well you remembered each card. Anki offers FSRS as an option alongside its SM-2 based scheduler.

### Is Anki free on iPhone?

The iPhone and iPad app, AnkiMobile, is a one-time $24.99 purchase. Anki on desktop and AnkiDroid on Android are free.

### Can BrainLift work offline like Anki?

BrainLift's AI features, such as quiz generation and tutor chat, need an internet connection. If offline study matters most to you, Anki is the better fit.
