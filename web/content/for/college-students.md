College exams lean heavily on two sources: what the professor said in lecture and what was in the assigned reading. Midterms and finals often ask you to apply a concept to a new example, so recognizing a term on a slide is not enough. You need practice answering questions before the exam does it for you.

## Where studying in college breaks down

### The readings are longer than your week

Three courses can easily assign a few hundred pages a week. Most students skim, then panic before the midterm. Upload a reading as a PDF and BrainLift generates a quiz on its main ideas, so you find out what you actually absorbed and where to go back.

### Several finals land at once

When four exams fall in the same week, you cannot give each one a full review. Keep a deck for every course and let spaced repetition handle the scheduling. Cards you know well wait; cards you keep missing come back sooner, across all your classes.

### Professors' slides leave gaps

Some slides are just headings and a chart, with the real explanation said out loud. When your notes stop making sense, open the AI tutor chat and ask it to walk through the idea step by step. It can sketch a chart or diagram when a graph explains it better than a paragraph.

## What a generated quiz looks like for college

This sample comes from an intro microeconomics lecture on markets.

**1. A binding price ceiling set below the equilibrium price causes:**
A) A surplus
B) A shortage (correct)
C) No change in quantity
D) A rise in the market price
*Why:* At the capped price, buyers want more than sellers are willing to supply.

**2. A 10% rise in price leads to a 20% drop in quantity demanded. Demand is:**
A) Elastic (correct)
B) Inelastic
C) Unit elastic
D) Perfectly inelastic
*Why:* The percentage change in quantity is larger than the percentage change in price, giving an elasticity of 2.

**3. Which definition fits opportunity cost?**
A) The money spent on a purchase
B) The value of the next-best alternative given up (correct)
C) The total cost of production
D) A cost that cannot be recovered
*Why:* Every choice gives up something, and opportunity cost measures the most valuable option you passed on.

| Front | Back |
|---|---|
| Binding price floor (above equilibrium) | Creates a surplus |
| GDP by expenditure | C + I + G + NX |
| Marginal cost | The change in total cost from producing one more unit |
| Law of demand | As price rises, quantity demanded falls, other things equal |

Professors define terms in slightly different ways, so compare generated answers with your lecture notes and syllabus readings.

## Study habits that work in college

Treat every lecture as a small test. Within a day, generate ten questions on it and answer them without your notes. A meta-analysis of practice testing found that it generally produced more learning than restudying the same material ([Adesope et al., 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654316689306)).

Plan finals backward. Give the earliest exam the most time first, while keeping a short spaced review going for the others.
