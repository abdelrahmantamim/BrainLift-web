Writing flashcards by hand eats into the time you could spend studying them, so BrainLift does the drafting for you. Paste or type your notes, get a deck back, and spend your time on recall instead of on index cards.

## How it works

1. **Paste or type your notes.** You drop in text from class, a study guide, or a document, and it shows up in the input box exactly as you entered it.
2. **Create the deck.** BrainLift splits the material into cards, and you see each one as a prompt on the front and a short answer on the back.
3. **Study with spaced repetition.** You flip through the deck, rate each card by how well you recalled it, and cards you rated hard return before cards you rated easy.

## What you get

Here is a realistic example from typed anatomy notes on muscles of the upper and lower limb.

| Line in your notes | Flashcard BrainLift drafts |
|---|---|
| Biceps brachii: inserts on radial tuberosity, flexes elbow and supinates forearm | Front: What are the two main actions of the biceps brachii? Back: Elbow flexion and forearm supination |
| Deltoid: main abductor of the arm, innervated by axillary nerve | Front: Which nerve innervates the deltoid? Back: The axillary nerve |
| Quadriceps femoris: extends the knee, femoral nerve | Front: What action do the quadriceps perform at the knee? Back: Knee extension |
| Diaphragm: phrenic nerve, roots C3 to C5 | Front: Which nerve supplies the diaphragm, and from which spinal roots? Back: The phrenic nerve, C3 to C5 |

Each note line becomes a card that asks you to produce the answer, which is harder and more useful than recognizing it on a page.

## Tips for better results

- **Put one idea per line or bullet.** Dense paragraphs can turn into cards that test three things at once. Short, separate statements become cleaner cards.
- **Keep each deck to one subject.** A deck for "upper limb muscles" is easier to schedule and review than one that mixes anatomy, physiology, and pharmacology.
- **Include the "why" when it matters.** Cards that only test a name are quick to forget. Adding a cause, function, or comparison to your notes gives the AI more to work with.
- **Delete cards you already know cold.** Reviewing facts you will never miss wastes time, so trim them after the first pass.
- **Add a quiz when you want exam practice.** Flashcards build recall of single facts. For mixed questions, generate a quiz from the same notes and set the question count and difficulty to match your exam.

## Accuracy

AI sometimes drops a qualifier, swaps a detail, or writes a back side that is too vague to grade yourself on. Read through a new deck once with your notes open and correct anything that does not match before you start rating cards. Spacing those reviews out is worth the effort: in one study, flashcards studied in a single large spaced deck were remembered better than the same cards crammed in smaller batches, even though learners believed cramming worked better ([Kornell, 2009](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1361699994055985152)).

## FAQ

### What kind of notes can I use?

Typed or pasted text works directly, and you can also make flashcards from a photo of your notes or from a PDF. Notes from any app work as long as you can copy the text.

### How much text should I paste at once?

A single lecture or topic is a good amount and keeps the deck focused. Very long pastes can produce a deck that is hard to review, and usage limits depend on your plan.

### Can I turn handwritten notes into flashcards?

Yes. Photograph the page and BrainLift reads the handwriting, though tidy writing works noticeably better than quick scribbles, so check those cards against your page.

### Can I make flashcards from notes on the web?

Yes, the BrainLift web app works in a browser on any computer. The iPhone and iPad app does the same, and Android users can use the web app since there is no Android app yet.
