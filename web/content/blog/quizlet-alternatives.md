Quizlet is still the app most students know, and its library of shared sets is enormous. But many students now hit limits on the free plan and start looking around. Here are nine alternatives, with an honest take on what each does well and where it falls short.

## Why students are leaving Quizlet

The most common reasons are about the free plan and features:

- **Study modes are limited on the free plan.** Learn mode is restricted for free users, according to third-party reports. Check [Quizlet's plans](https://quizlet.com/upgrade) for current limits.
- **The AI tutor changed.** Q-Chat, Quizlet's AI tutor, was reported discontinued after June 30, 2025.
- **Students want cards from their own material.** Typing notes into sets takes time, and shared sets vary in accuracy.

None of this makes Quizlet a bad tool. If you mainly study from existing sets with classmates, it may still be the right choice.

## The 9 alternatives

### 1. Knowt

Knowt is the closest one-for-one swap. The free plan includes unlimited file creation, a free Learn mode (multiple choice, true/false, fill-in, flashcards), practice tests, spaced repetition, and import from Quizlet. Ultra costs $149.99 per year or $24.99 per month and adds ad-free study, AI summarizers, and full AP, SAT, and ACT prep.

- **Good fit for:** students who want Quizlet-like features free.
- **Drawback:** the free plan has ads.

See [BrainLift vs Knowt](/compare/brainlift-vs-knowt/) for a closer look.

### 2. Anki

Anki is free and open source on desktop and Android. AnkiMobile for iPhone is a $24.99 one-time purchase. It offers the FSRS scheduler as an option, thousands of shared decks, add-ons, and offline study.

- **Good fit for:** power users who want full control and serious spaced repetition.
- **Drawback:** a real learning curve, and card creation is manual by default.

### 3. Brainscape

Brainscape uses confidence-based repetition, where you rate each card from 1 to 5. The free Basic plan includes unlimited typed cards, import, sharing, reminders, and access to 2,500 preloaded cards. It can make AI cards from files and photos. Pro unlocks unlimited AI cards; see the [pricing page](https://www.brainscape.com/pricing) for the current price.

- **Good fit for:** exam prep with expert-certified decks, such as nursing or bar exams.
- **Drawback:** much of the premade library sits behind Pro.

### 4. BrainLift

BrainLift turns photos of notes, PDFs, typed notes, or a topic into quizzes and flashcard decks. When you miss quiz questions, "practice my mistakes" turns them into a deck. It also has an AI tutor chat, spaced repetition with self-rating, XP, daily quests, and streaks, and you can share quizzes with friends. It is free to start with an optional Pro plan, and runs on iPhone, iPad, and the web.

- **Good fit for:** turning your own class material into practice fast, and fixing what you got wrong.
- **Drawback:** no Android app yet, and no large public library of shared sets.

If snapping a photo of your notes sounds easier than typing a set, you can [try BrainLift free](/auth/register).

### 5. StudyFetch

StudyFetch is an AI study suite. It includes an AI companion, a tutor mode, a study planner with a cram mode, live lecture recording, notes, flashcards, quizzes, and practice tests. It runs on web, iOS, and Android. It has a free tier and paid plans; check [StudyFetch's site](https://www.studyfetch.com/) for current pricing.

- **Good fit for:** students who want lecture recording and a study calendar in one app.
- **Drawback:** lots of features can mean more to learn.

See [BrainLift vs StudyFetch](/compare/brainlift-vs-studyfetch/) for how the two differ.

### 6. RemNote

RemNote combines note-taking with flashcards. You write notes and turn lines into cards that enter a spaced repetition queue. It has a free plan and paid upgrades; check its site for details.

- **Good fit for:** students who want notes and review in one tool.
- **Drawback:** it works best if you take your notes inside it, which is a bigger habit change.

### 7. Kahoot!

Kahoot! is a game-style quiz platform used in many classrooms. It is built for live, competitive quizzes rather than solo spaced review.

- **Good fit for:** group review sessions and study groups.
- **Drawback:** not designed for long-term spaced repetition.

### 8. A general AI chatbot

You can paste notes into a general AI chatbot and ask for practice questions. It is flexible and often free to try.

- **Good fit for:** quick one-off quizzes on a topic.
- **Drawback:** no review schedule, no saved decks by default, and you have to check answers for errors.

### 9. Paper cards in a Leitner box

Index cards and a few labeled boxes still work. Cards you get right move to a box you review less often. Cards you miss go back to box one.

- **Good fit for:** anyone who studies better away from a phone.
- **Drawback:** slow to make, hard to carry, no search.

## How to move your Quizlet sets

You do not have to start over. Here is a simple path:

1. **Pick your keepers.** Only move sets for classes you are still taking or exams coming up.
2. **Use a direct import if there is one.** Knowt offers Quizlet import. Some other apps accept copied text.
3. **Export as text otherwise.** Copy your set's terms and definitions as plain text with a clear separator, such as a tab between term and definition.
4. **Clean up after importing.** Fix formatting, split cards that hold two ideas, and delete cards you already know cold.
5. **Turn on spaced review.** The tool matters less than the habit. Practice testing and spaced practice are two of the highest-rated study techniques in a major review of learning research ([Dunlosky et al., 2013](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)).

## FAQ

### What is the closest free alternative to Quizlet?

Knowt is the closest match. Its free plan includes Learn mode, practice tests, spaced repetition, and Quizlet import, with ads.

### Is Anki better than Quizlet?

It depends on what you want. Anki gives you more control and stronger spaced repetition, while Quizlet is easier to start with and has a huge library of shared sets.

### Which Quizlet alternative makes flashcards from photos of notes?

Several apps now make cards from images. Brainscape can create AI cards from photos, and BrainLift turns photos of notes into both quizzes and flashcard decks.

### Is Quizlet's Q-Chat gone?

Q-Chat was reported discontinued after June 30, 2025. Check Quizlet's own site for its current AI features.

### Can I use Quizlet and another app together?

Yes. Many students keep Quizlet for shared class sets and use a second app for their own notes or spaced review.
