High school tests mostly check whether you can remember and explain what was covered in class over the last few weeks. Your teacher's notes and handouts usually give you a good preview of what will be on the test. The trick is turning them into practice before the night before.

## Where studying for high school tests breaks down

### Tests sneak up on you

One week you have nothing due, and the next week you have a history test, a chemistry quiz, and an essay. Snapping a photo of your notes takes seconds, and BrainLift turns it into a practice quiz right away, so getting started is never the hard part.

### Rereading notes is boring and does not stick

Reading the same page three times feels like studying, but on test day the words in front of you are gone. Answering questions forces you to pull the information out of your own head. Each quiz in BrainLift explains the right answer, and the questions you miss can become flashcards with "practice my mistakes."

### Studying alone is hard to keep up

It is easy to skip a day when nobody notices. BrainLift tracks streaks, gives you daily quests, and awards XP as you climb through 10 levels and unlock achievements. You can also send a quiz to friends with a link or QR code and see who knows the material.

## What a generated quiz looks like for high school

Here is a sample from a set of World History notes on the causes of World War I.

**1. Which event directly triggered the start of World War I in 1914?**
A) The sinking of the Lusitania
B) The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand (correct)
C) The signing of the Treaty of Versailles
D) The Russian Revolution
*Why:* The archduke was shot in Sarajevo in June 1914, and the alliance system turned that crisis into a wider war.

**2. Historians often summarize the long-term causes as MAIN. Which of these is NOT one of them?**
A) Militarism
B) Alliances
C) Isolationism (correct)
D) Nationalism
*Why:* MAIN stands for militarism, alliances, imperialism, and nationalism.

**3. Which countries made up the Triple Entente?**
A) Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy
B) France, Russia, and Great Britain (correct)
C) Britain, the United States, and Japan
D) Germany, the Ottoman Empire, and Bulgaria
*Why:* France, Russia, and Britain formed the Entente; Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy formed the Triple Alliance.

| Front | Back |
|---|---|
| Year World War I began | 1914 |
| Treaty that ended the war with Germany | Treaty of Versailles (1919) |
| Central Powers | Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, and Bulgaria |
| Zimmermann Telegram | A 1917 German message proposing a military alliance with Mexico against the United States |

If anything here differs from what your teacher covered, go with your class notes and textbook.

## Study habits that work for high school

Start three or four days before a test, not the night before. Do one short quiz each day, and focus on the questions you got wrong the time before. Spreading out your practice like this makes the information last longer than one long cram session.

Quizzing yourself works even when it feels harder than reading. In one study, students who practiced recalling material learned more than students who built concept maps from it ([Karpicke and Blunt, 2011](https://www.purdue.edu/newsroom/research/2011/110120KarpickeScience.html)).
