BrainLift takes the notes you already have, typed or pasted, and writes a practice quiz from them. You decide how hard it is and how many questions you get, so the quiz can be a quick check after class or a full practice test before an exam.

## How it works

1. **Add your notes.** You paste text from your notes app or document, or type it in, and you see the full text in the input box before you generate anything.
2. **Set difficulty and length.** You choose a difficulty level and the number of questions, so a quick review and a longer practice test come from the same notes.
3. **Take the quiz and review.** You answer each question, see which ones you got right, and can send your wrong answers to a "practice my mistakes" flashcard deck that includes explanations.

## What you get

Here is a realistic example from typed general chemistry notes.

| Line in your notes | Practice question BrainLift drafts |
|---|---|
| Avogadro's number = 6.022 x 10^23 particles per mole | How many particles are in one mole of a substance? |
| Ionic bonds: electrons transferred, usually metal + nonmetal | In an ionic bond, what happens to the electrons between the two atoms? |
| pH = negative log of [H+]; pure water at 25 C has pH 7 | What is the pH of pure water at 25 C, and what does that value tell you? |
| Exothermic: releases heat, enthalpy change is negative | If a reaction releases heat to its surroundings, what is the sign of its enthalpy change? |

At a higher difficulty, expect questions that ask you to apply an idea, such as predicting the bond type for a given pair of elements, instead of only recalling a definition.

## Tips for better results

- **Group notes by topic before you paste.** One quiz per unit or lecture keeps questions focused. Mixing three chapters into one paste spreads the questions thin.
- **Write in phrases, not lone keywords.** "Mitosis" alone gives the AI little to work with, while "mitosis makes two identical diploid cells" gives it a fact to test.
- **Clean up shorthand only you understand.** Personal abbreviations can be misread. Spell out anything a classmate would not recognize.
- **Choose question count by the time you have.** A short quiz works for a daily check, and a longer one makes sense when you want a practice test closer to exam day.
- **Raise the difficulty as you improve.** Start at an easier level while the material is new, then move up once you are scoring well, so the quiz keeps stretching you.

## Accuracy

The quiz is only as reliable as the notes behind it, and the AI can still write a question with a wrong answer key or an answer choice that is also defensible. If a result surprises you, go back to your notes or textbook rather than trusting the quiz, and fix or skip that question. Practice tests are still worth building: a meta-analysis of practice testing research found that taking practice tests generally improved learning compared with other ways of studying ([Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan, 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654316689306)).

## FAQ

### What can I use as input?

You can paste or type notes, and BrainLift can also build quizzes from photos of notes, PDFs, or just a topic name. For a typed workflow, plain text from any notes app or document works well.

### How long can my notes be?

There is no need to paste a whole semester at once, and very long text tends to produce less focused quizzes. A lecture or chapter at a time is a good size, and limits may vary by plan.

### Can I use handwritten notes?

Yes, take a photo of the page instead of typing it. BrainLift reads handwriting, though clear writing gives better results, so double check any question that seems off.

### Does it work in a browser?

Yes, the web app runs in your browser on a laptop or desktop, which is handy when your notes are already in a document. There is also an iPhone and iPad app, but no Android app yet.
